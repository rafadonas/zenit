## Organization context

Pages 1-7 introduce the FIAP open-innovation program and Motiva. They describe a
mobility and infrastructure group operating road, rail, and airport concessions and
present organizational purpose, culture, footprint, and economic context.

Those pages are background for the academic challenge. Counts of concessions,
kilometers, cities, employees, passengers, vehicles, or cargo shown in the deck are
source-date promotional figures, not ZENIT metrics and not maintained here as current
facts.

## Conservation context

Pages 8-11 position conservation and maintenance as recurring and emergency work to
preserve road assets and operations. The deck names examples such as mowing, drainage
maintenance, and earthwork maintenance and places the concessionaire between the
granting authority, shareholders, and road users.

The source also points to ARTESP and ANTT contractual interfaces. ZENIT must not
combine those regimes as if every clause applied to every road. The applicable road,
concession, contract, and regulator must be identified before operational use.

## Vegetation-monitoring challenge

Pages 12-14 define the central challenge: monitor and manage roadside vegetation more
efficiently using remote sensing, data analysis, or artificial intelligence to support
planning and intervention decisions.

The problem framing emphasizes:

- dependence on manual inspection and periodic maintenance;
- difficulty maintaining continuous awareness across long corridors;
- risk of intervention too early or too late;
- opportunity to consolidate evidence and prioritize attention; and
- possible benefits in resource use, planning, maintenance history, and monitoring.

Illustrative roadside and finishing-mowing images show the physical context. They are
not labeled training data, measurements, ground truth, or proof of current conditions.

## ZENIT interpretation

ZENIT answers the challenge with an auditable decision-support chain rather than an
autonomous mowing system:

```text
dated source evidence
  -> 100 m segment and separate zone
  -> quality-aware analysis
  -> explainable recommendation
  -> explicit human review
  -> prepared field workflow
  -> reviewed evidence and traceable report
```

The academic prototype may demonstrate the chain and discuss potential benefits. It
must not claim measured savings, safety improvement, continuous monitoring, or
operational optimization without a defined baseline and evaluated pilot.

## Requirements derived for the project

- maintain a consolidated spatial and temporal view;
- make evidence age and quality visible;
- use remote sensing to prioritize, not to fabricate exact height;
- preserve human control over interventions;
- connect recommendation, review, field evidence, and history;
- support reproducible academic evaluation; and
- distinguish source statements, project decisions, simulations, and future work.
